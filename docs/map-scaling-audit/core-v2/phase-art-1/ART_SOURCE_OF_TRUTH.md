## Shared traits

| Trait | Locked observation | Map consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Painting/detail | Polished hand-painted forms with crisp readable silhouettes, selective fine detail, and slightly dimensional volume | Terrain uses coherent painterly surfaces and macro variation; it must not look like blurred stamps or repeated tiles |
| Camera | Fixed elevated three-quarter isometric/orthographic presentation with upright structures and grounded footprints | Terrain marks, props, roads, walls, fields, and rocks use the same projection and apparent scale |
| Lighting | Soft natural daylight with a restrained upper/front-side key, clear material planes, and no theatrical effects | All Core maps share one daylight logic; climate changes color and moisture, not sun direction |
| Contrast | Strong object silhouette and internal midtone separation, with limited bright accents | Background local contrast stays below runtime-object contrast so cities remain dominant |
| Materials | Rough fieldstone, dressed stone on prestige structures, timber framing, clay tile, slate, canvas, iron, leather, dirt, and worn grass | Environment props use practical late-medieval materials and show construction logic |
| Saturation | Earth, moss, wood, parchment, stone, muted burgundy, and faded indigo; gilt is rare | No neon green, magical blue, literal gold terrain, or oversaturated biome color |
| Shadows | Compact attached contact shadows and ambient occlusion ground each transparent object | Terrain props use short soft shadows; no long dramatic cast shadows that fight overlays |
| Weathering | Chipped stone, repaired timber, worn roads, imperfect cloth, smoke, mud, ruts, and human use | Core terrain feels maintained or worked where appropriate, but never factory-perfect |
| Visual scale | Cities progress within one fixed family; Camps, Strongholds, and Citadel occupy distinct fixed gameplay classes | Background props stay subordinate to 66–78 px cities, 132 px Camps, 154 px Strongholds, and the 260 px Citadel |
| Realism | Grounded 14th–15th century frontier Europe filtered through polished game art | Believability and gameplay clarity outrank fantasy spectacle or photorealism |

## Structure-to-environment connection

The approved objects contain dense authored detail inside compact footprints. Their surrounding terrain therefore needs broad quiet areas, restrained low-frequency texture, and believable roads/fields that continue the same stone, timber, earth, and vegetation language. The map supports the structures; it does not compete with them.

City growth also establishes the density hierarchy. Small rural cues may appear outside the center, but no background cottage cluster may resemble another runtime city. Objective territory identity comes from a few environmental clues while the actual Camp, Stronghold, or Citadel supplies the focal silhouette.

## Non-authoritative prototype findings

The Phase A/B1 backgrounds retain valid layout, density, road, objective-placement, and topology evidence. Their repeated foundation texture, hard compositing seams, broad soft roads, and abrupt climate blocks do not match the source structures' final-art finish. That distinction is an art-only finding and does not revoke the prior runtime approvals.
