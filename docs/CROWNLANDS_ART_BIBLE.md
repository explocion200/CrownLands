# Crownlands Art Bible

This document is the visual source of truth for Crownlands. It anchors the game in a grounded 14th-15th century Western and Central European frontier kingdom. Crownlands is fictional, but the physical world should feel built by human labor: roads, walls, harvests, merchants, craftsmen, soldiers, horses, timber, stone, iron, banners, farms, trade, local rulers, and ordinary people.

## North Star

Crownlands should feel beautiful, prosperous, fragile, weathered, defensible, vulnerable, and human. It should not read as a generic fantasy mobile kingdom game. Fantasy and special effects are allowed only when gameplay needs them, and then they should be expressed through mist, dust, candlelight, smoke, wax, ink, worn metal, manuscript marks, or restrained ceremonial shimmer.

## Historical Target

The broad anchor is 1350-1500 frontier Europe. Do not copy one historical kingdom exactly. Build a believable Crownlands culture from late-medieval materials, practical military architecture, hand-made heraldry, administrative records, rural production, trade, and local noble authority.

## Material Language

Primary materials:

- Rough fieldstone, repaired masonry, dressed stone on major buildings.
- Timber framing, oak beams, wattle and daub, limewashed plaster.
- Thatch, wood shingles, clay roof tile only on richer structures.
- Packed dirt, mud, straw, road ruts, soot, rain staining.
- Worn iron, hammered iron, dull steel, aged brass, restrained gilt.
- Leather, rope, wool, linen, canvas, parchment, vellum, wax.
- Carved wood, hand-painted cloth, simple stained glass.

Nothing should look factory perfect. Preferred surface detail includes chipped plaster, crooked construction, patched stone, repaired wood, worn edges, uneven stitching, tarnished metal, faded paint, and hand-tool marks.

## Architecture

Buildings should look believable and grown over time:

- Irregular footprints, asymmetrical additions, small windows, shutters.
- Stone foundations, timber frames, limewashed infill, rough masonry.
- Sheds, lean-tos, fences, wells, yards, carts, barrels, firewood.
- Workshops, market stalls, animal pens, gardens, farms, chimneys.

Avoid perfect radial cities, pristine castles, impossible scale, oversized towers, and generic fantasy architecture.

## Fortifications

Fortifications are practical first:

- Palisades, ditches, earthworks, reinforced gates at low levels.
- Curtain walls, stone towers, gatehouses, hoardings, defensive yards at higher levels.
- Defense Stronghold should emphasize layered approach, thicker walls, earthworks, and towers.
- Crown Citadel should feel richer and more carefully built, not magical.

## Settlement Life

Every settlement should communicate labor and daily life:

- Chimney smoke, worn roads, muddy cart tracks, hay, grain sacks.
- Livestock, gardens, timber stacks, blacksmith yards, market stalls.
- Bridges, crossings, roadside shrines, mills where appropriate.
- Crowded housing near important centers.

Cities and castles should not feel like isolated tokens placed on empty decorative terrain.

## Characters

Characters are grounded late-medieval officers, stewards, and commanders.

Materials and equipment:

- Wool, linen, leather, mail, padded cloth, simple metal armor.
- Gambesons, mail, brigandine, kettle helmets, bascinets, sallets.
- Spears, swords, polearms, bows, crossbows, shields.

Avoid giant pauldrons, glowing armor, huge swords, spikes, superhero silhouettes, excessive skulls, and fantasy knight treatment for every officer.

## Crowns

Crowns should feel like crafted metal objects: simple gold or gilt circlets, fleur-inspired points, modest gems on elite pieces, visible hammering, engraving, and slight imperfections. Avoid oversized high-fantasy artifacts.

## Heraldry

Heraldry should look hand made:

- Simple charges: animals, towers, crowns, crosses, stars, crops, tools, weapons, geometric divisions.
- Cloth banners should show seams, wrinkles, stitching, sun fading, hand-painted marks, and wear.
- Clan shields should feel like painted wood, cloth, leather, or aged metal.

Avoid esports logos, corporate vector marks, magical crests, perfect symmetry, and modern flat branding.

## UI Philosophy

The player should feel like a ruler interacting with physical administrative and military objects:

- Charters, ledgers, campaign maps, proclamations, wax seals.
- Wooden boards, iron fittings, leather folders, cloth banners.
- Military orders, troop tokens, treasury records, quartermaster stores.

Do not simply put parchment texture behind modern cards. Layout, controls, borders, icons, and animations should all support the material fiction.

## Panel Families

Use different material families by purpose:

| Purpose | Treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Administrative | Parchment, ledger sheets, ruled lines, ink, wax stamps. |
| Military | Dark wood, iron reinforcement, leather, nailed frames, cloth unit markers. |
| Royal | Higher quality parchment, burgundy cloth, carved details, muted gilt, seals. |
| Economy | Ledgers, coin scales, stamped parchment, counting boards, wooden desks. |
| Clan | Banners, shields, stitched cloth, heraldic panels. |
| Combat | Campaign parchment, troop tokens, route annotations, seals, dispatch orders. |
| Scouting | Intelligence reports, field sketches, uncertainty marks, faded stale information. |

## Corners And Shapes

Reduce modern rounded cards. Default radius should be practical: 1-4px, up to 8px only for repeated item cards or touch targets. Use clipped parchment edges, wood frames, iron brackets, cloth tabs, and squared panels.

## Buttons

Button treatments:

- Military: iron-edged wood or stamped leather.
- Administrative: parchment tab or stamped ledger control.
- Primary confirmation: burgundy cloth or painted wood.
- Danger: dark rust or oxblood.
- Disabled: faded parchment, tarnished metal, desaturated cloth.

Buttons must stay readable, tappable, and responsive on mobile.

## Typography

Current fonts are Cinzel, Cinzel Decorative, and IM Fell English. Use them with restraint:

- Ceremonial titles: Cinzel Decorative, sparingly.
- Major headings: Cinzel or a readable historic serif.
- Body and dense UI: readable serif or system fallback when numbers and compact labels matter.
- Numbers/timers: tabular numerals, clarity first.

Do not use blackletter-style display for body copy or compact UI.

## Manuscript Influence

Use subtle late-medieval manuscript accents:

- Ruled parchment, red rubrication, small botanical flourishes.
- Occasional illuminated initials or decorative chapter headers.
- Wax seals, ink marks, chronicle entries.

Do not turn every screen into a dense illuminated manuscript.

## Iconography

Replace emoji/unicode-style icons over time with a cohesive Crownlands icon system:

- Woodcut silhouettes, ink illustrations, stamped metal, manuscript pictograms.
- Consistent stroke weight, limited detail, clear at mobile HUD sizes.
- Use symbols plus shape/pattern for gameplay state, not color alone.

Priority replacements: sword, horse, telescope/scouting, crown, shield, castle, scroll, music, fullscreen, navigation arrows, incoming and outgoing army glyphs.

## Global HUD, Loading, And Installed Identity Standards

The high-frequency interface is a compact ruler's command system made from physical Crownlands materials. Navigation uses dark oak, iron fittings, parchment records, leather, burgundy cloth, faded indigo, oxblood wax, and muted brass. Do not frame every control as an ornate object; royal identity, resources, military warnings, and practical navigation must remain visibly distinct.

Canonical source and runtime dimensions:

- Standard HUD icon source: 1254x1254 RGBA; fixed transparent runtime canvas: 192x192 WebP.
- Daily Reward source: 1254x1254 RGBA WebP; fixed transparent runtime canvas: 160x160 WebP.
- Profile frame source: 1419x1108 RGBA; fixed transparent runtime canvas: 256x200 WebP.
- Map-switch arrow source: 654x720 RGBA; fixed transparent runtime canvas: 192x212 WebP.
- Loading ring and crown sources: 1254x1254 RGBA; fixed transparent runtime canvases: 256x256 WebP.
- PWA identity master: 1254x1254 opaque RGB. Derive normal and maskable 192x192 and 512x512 PNGs plus a dedicated 32x32 favicon. Keep the crown-and-gate emblem within the central maskable safe area.
- HUD, daily, loading, and navigation derivatives preserve their transparent fixed canvases; optimization must not tightly trim them.

Icon family:

- Use readable physical objects or stamped pictograms: ranked heraldic standards, settlement ledger, folded road map, merchant scale, leather satchel, sealed dispatch, deed seal, and steward calendar.
- All icons share a slightly elevated object view, warm upper-left daylight, natural ambient shadow, restrained edge wear, and a narrow detail range that survives 18-56px display.
- Avoid modern podiums, map pins, shopping carts, trophy cups, social-media bubbles, emoji, shiny rarity frames, and unrelated fantasy-castle silhouettes.
- The unified Crownlands crown is a simple hammered gilt form with modest points and no oversized gemstones. Loading, PWA identity, royal status, king indicators, and Citadel UI inherit this silhouette.
- Attack, transfer, scout, crown, gold, reports, fullscreen, sound, and navigation glyphs may remain code-native SVG where that improves clarity. They must use the Crownlands stamped/woodcut shape language and `currentColor`; scouting must not use a telescope.

HUD hierarchy and state:

- Ruler identity uses the compact carved-oak profile plaque. The player flag remains dominant inside it and the hero level is a small timber/iron tag.
- Gold uses a ledger/hammered-coin treatment, never premium-currency glow.
- Citadel and incoming-attack states use oxblood military dispatches. Outgoing/scout operations use faded-indigo dispatches. Normal navigation remains dark timber and iron.
- Unread counts are oxblood wax marks with high-contrast tabular numbers. Timers use compact parchment or iron-framed tags and readable system numerals.
- Default, hover, pressed, selected, unread, disabled, active, and warning states communicate through physical depth, darker timber, iron inset, cloth color, wax marks, and restrained motion. Glow is not a primary state signal.

Loading and travel:

- Loading uses a slowly rotating engraved iron ring around the unified Crownlands crown. The crown settles subtly but does not radiate light or particles.
- Loading panels use royal parchment within timber/iron framing. Reduced-motion mode removes rotation and pulsing without hiding status.
- Regional travel uses quick natural mist and desaturated cloud texture over physical map motion. Avoid magical smoke, energy wipes, and long transitions that delay play.

Installed identity:

- The app emblem is a simple hammered crown over a stone gate on weathered burgundy cloth. It contains no text and no secondary army/map symbols.
- Manifest colors are soot `#17110D` for the background and burgundy `#5A262B` for browser/install chrome.
- Favicon, Apple touch icon, normal PWA icons, maskable icons, loading crown, and royal HUD marks must read as one brand family.

## Map Art

### Regional World Map Standards

The 15 regional maps depict one continuous Crownlands continent, not separate fantasy islands or game boards.

- Authoritative runtime canvas: 1448x1086 opaque WebP for every region.
- Derived map-picker canvas: 320x240 opaque WebP. Thumbnails inherit the source palette and geography without independent filters or repainting.
- Camera: one elevated near-top-down strategy view with no horizon, shared physical scale, soft daylight from broadly the upper-left, restrained ambient haze, and consistent painterly detail density.
- Terrain palette: muted earth, ochre, olive, moss, straw, charcoal rock, weathered gray, and gray-green water. Regional identity comes from land use and geography, not heavy color filters.
- Human influence: developed land shows irregular strip fields, pasture, orchards, hedges, drainage, coppiced woodland, logging or quarry traces, muddy roads, ruts, bridges, fords, and maintained causeways.
- Runtime safety: normal placement zones remain free of baked cities, camps, Strongholds, objective pads, ritual rings, decorative plazas, labels, borders, and UI marks.

The world-layout and region JSON are authoritative for adjacency. Artwork never invents connectivity.

- Each open side has exactly one clear road, valley path, bridge approach, causeway, or trade route reaching that edge.
- Internal roads may branch, but only one branch may reach a given open side.
- A closed side has no road exit and is naturally blocked by continuous woodland, rocky ridge, steep ground, marsh, dense vegetation, or another believable feature.
- Connected edges should share plausible road scale, land use, elevation, water behavior, lighting, and vegetation. Pixel-perfect stitching is not required because travel uses a brief mist transition, but adjacent regions must remain geographically believable.
- Map edge routes remain broad enough to read under navigation arrows and subtle enough to feel like landscape rather than painted UI.

Regional progression is gradual. Crownlands Heart is the most cultivated administrative core; the royal cross remains prosperous and managed; the middle belt mixes woodland, agriculture, road, and trade functions; southern starter frontiers are rougher, wetter, hillier, or less cultivated without becoming exaggerated fantasy biomes.

The legacy `center-island.webp`, `east-island.webp`, `north-island.webp`, `south-island.webp`, and `west-island.webp` filenames are compatibility fallbacks for the map editor. They mirror their active regional sources and must not preserve an older visual family.

Regional travel uses a 1254x1254 RGBA natural-mist source and a 448x448 RGBA runtime WebP. The texture uses irregular neutral ivory and weathered-gray low fog with transparent openings; no ring, vortex, purple/blue tint, magical smoke, or particle effect.

### Pass 4A-R Correction

Pass 4A-R supersedes the Pass 4A production-art decision while retaining the map standards above as guidance for any future, separately approved map pass.

- The 15 authoritative regional backgrounds are the exact pre-Pass-4A files recovered from revision `f4604e98824d57e1f27fc9a3f8bcb014519366d0` and the Pass 4A rollback archive.
- The five root `*-island.webp` files retain their exact pre-Pass-4A compatibility artwork. They are a separate legacy/editor class and are not required to be byte-identical to the active royal-region backgrounds.
- Regional thumbnails use the restored pre-Pass-4A 420x315 source/versioned contract and are always fingerprinted from the current authoritative thumbnail files.
- Regional travel uses the exact pre-Pass-4A 1254x1254 RGBA cloud source and its 448x448 optimized derivative. It remains pale, natural, non-magical, and brief.
- Any future regional repaint requires explicit review. Optimization must never silently replace or reinterpret these corrected source masters.

All regional maps must look like one connected continent. Shared rules:

- Same camera distance, terrain scale, road width, river width, and settlement scale.
- Natural terrain, overcast or warm daylight, grounded water treatment.
- Roads are muddy, rutted, irregular, and more worn near cities.
- Farmland and trade infrastructure appear near developed regions.
- Coasts and rivers are gray-blue/green-gray, not magical or glossy.
- Forests, mountains, ruins, bridges, fog, and decorative density must be consistent.

## Map Overlays

Gameplay state must remain instantly readable but grounded:

- Player: parchment/ochre selected marks plus banner/shape.
- Enemy: oxblood/rust plus warning mark or jagged/torn banner.
- Clan ally: moss green plus allied badge/pattern.
- Neutral: weathered gray plus plain marker.
- Selected: ink ring, brass pin, or chalk circle.
- Routes: ink strokes, stitched ribbon, chalk annotations, moving dust.
- Scouts: dotted ink, field-sketch radius, faded uncertainty.
- Incoming: red wax, torn dispatch, warning bell, marching marker.

Do not rely on color alone.

## City Progression

City art must show organic settlement growth:

1. Frontier holding: timber house, rough palisade, sheds, mud, wood piles.
2. Fortified village: more buildings, stronger palisade, small stone, workshops, fields.
3. Keep settlement: stone keep, gate, denser housing, market, early walls.
4. Fortified town: stronger stone defenses, towers, dense housing, trade area.
5. Major city: significant but believable walls, gatehouse, civic/noble structures, smoke, roads, markets, workshops.

Stage 5 must not become a fantasy megacity.

City cutouts must remain compact and self-contained. Do not place bushes, detached trees, loose props, or decorative terrain outside the defended settlement footprint. Limited vegetation and activity may remain inside the walls. Each successive stage must increase in both apparent width and height at gameplay size, with stronger vertical architecture and denser construction. Stage 5 must be visibly taller and more substantial than Stage 4, not only wider.

## Objectives

Strongholds share architecture but differ by function:

- Gold Stronghold: treasury/trade center, protected storehouses, merchant yards, guarded gates; not covered in literal gold.
- Training Stronghold: barracks, drill yard, archery, armory, tents, weapon racks.
- Speed Stronghold: stables, couriers, horse yards, road junction, relay infrastructure.
- Defense Stronghold: heavier fortification, towers, gatehouse, earthworks.
- Crown Citadel: larger, richer, more ceremonial, more fortified; restrained gilt only.

Camps:

- Gold Camp: merchant/tax convoy camp.
- Warband Camp: temporary military encampment.
- Relic Camp: shrine/reliquary/pavilion/candles/guarded sacred object, no neon magic.
- Deed Camp: land charter, wax seal, survey markers, records chest.

## Map Objective Scale Standards

The map-object scale system is a gameplay readability rule, not an incidental byproduct of transparent trimming. Future objective art must preserve its class canvas and safe area so matching objects read as the same gameplay size.

City:

- City stages remain governed by progression scale rather than the objective-site scale classes.
- Runtime city art sizes are Stage 1: 66px, Stage 2: 69px, Stage 3: 72px, Stage 4: 75px, Stage 5: 78px.
- Mobile landscape compact sizes are Stage 1: 61px, Stage 2: 64px, Stage 3: 67px, Stage 4: 70px, Stage 5: 73px.
- Canonical city source canvas: 768x768 RGBA PNG with intentional transparent padding.
- Corrected visible source heights are Stage 1: 500px, Stage 2: 555px, Stage 3: 610px, Stage 4: 645px, and Stage 5: 750px.
- Canonical optimized city canvas: fixed 256x256 WebP with alpha. Optimizers must preserve this canvas and must not tightly crop transparent padding.
- City progression uses both the increasing visible occupancy inside the fixed canvas and the existing staged CSS render sizes. This makes every stage grow upward and outward while preserving stable map hit areas.
- Keep the silhouette within the canvas edge, centered horizontally, grounded consistently, and free of detached exterior scenery. Shadows and the short gate road must remain attached to the main footprint.

Camp:

- Canonical source master canvas: 640x640 PNG with alpha.
- Canonical optimized runtime canvas: 384x384 WebP with alpha.
- Canonical runtime display size: 132x132px.
- All Camps must share the same source canvas, optimized canvas, runtime render size, visual footprint class, ground footprint, and shadow footprint.
- Camp artwork should occupy roughly 72-78% of the source canvas width and 58-68% of the source canvas height. Keep the visual mass visibly smaller than Strongholds.

Stronghold:

- Canonical source master canvas: 640x640 PNG with alpha.
- Canonical optimized runtime canvas: 384x384 WebP with alpha.
- Canonical runtime display size: 154x154px.
- Gold, Training, Speed, and Defense Strongholds must share the same source canvas, optimized canvas, runtime render size, visual footprint class, ground footprint, and shadow footprint.
- Different silhouettes are allowed only when they communicate function; they must not make one Stronghold appear to belong to a larger or smaller map-object class.

Crown Citadel:

- Canonical optimized runtime canvas: 384x384 WebP with alpha.
- Canonical runtime display size: 260x260px.
- The Crown Citadel is intentionally a larger class than Strongholds. It may be richer, taller, and broader, but it must still share Crownlands camera, lighting, material culture, and transparent standalone map-object framing.

Transparent padding and optimization:

- Transparent padding is intentional for Camps, Strongholds, and the Crown Citadel. Do not trim it away during optimization.
- Shadows, small ground patches, and road/courtyard grounding must remain inside the canonical canvas.
- Labels, owner markers, selection rings, hold rings, action wheels, troop displays, and reward indicators should be designed around these fixed boxes.
- Do not add per-object render-size overrides for individual Camp or Stronghold artwork unless a gameplay feature requires it and the exception is documented here.

## Items And Rewards

Consumables must be real objects:

- Royal Peace Shield: royal shield/proclamation/seal, not an energy bubble.
- War Drums: hide drum, painted mark, wooden sticks.
- Royal Tax Decree: parchment charter, royal wax seal, treasury mark, restrained coins.
- Veil of Silence: espionage/secrecy object, dark cloth, coded note, sealed packet.
- Swift March Order: folded dispatch, courier seal, leather case.
- Recall Horn: practical horn with carved/metal fittings and military cord.

Daily rewards should feel like a royal calendar, household ledger, or steward's record. Claimed states use wax stamps or ink marks; locked states use faded ink or tied cord.

## Inner Castle

The Inner Castle is a coherent royal compound. Treasury, Great Hall, Barracks, Alehouse, Gatehouse, Royal Stables, and hub must share stone culture, timber, roof materials, lighting, camera, and decorative language. Each building remains functionally distinct.

### Inner Castle Standards

The Inner Castle is a ruler's working royal bailey, not a menu collage. Future Inner Castle artwork must look like rooms and structures inside one prosperous Crownlands castle complex.

Canonical dimensions:

- Hub source master: 1448x1086 opaque PNG.
- Hub optimized runtime derivative: 1280x960 opaque WebP.
- Building scene source masters: 1254x1254 opaque PNG.
- Building optimized runtime derivatives: 512x512 opaque WebP.
- Hub runtime frame: 4:3 scene with existing hotspot percentages.
- Building runtime frame: square preview art inside the Inner Castle tray.

Hub layout and safe zones:

- Preserve the six hotspot zones unless the UI is deliberately migrated: Treasury 19%/24%, Great Hall 50%/20%, Barracks 81%/25%, Alehouse 19%/57%, Gatehouse 50%/75%, Royal Stables 81%/58%.
- Keep the central courtyard visually open enough for compact hotspot labels.
- Do not place faces, key props, or the only readable function cue directly beneath hotspot plaques.

Camera and composition:

- Hub: slightly elevated strategy-game view over a royal inner ward, about 35-45 degrees, same broad direction as the map-object family.
- Building scenes: consistent three-quarter location previews. Interiors may use room-corner perspective, but must share lens feel, human scale, masonry, timber, and exposure.
- Avoid mixing isometric tokens, cinematic low-angle paintings, flat front elevations, and unrelated interior concept art in the same family.

Lighting:

- Hub and exterior views use soft daylight with realistic shadows.
- Interiors use daylight through windows plus restrained hearth or candle light.
- Avoid strong orange fantasy glow, glowing treasure, magical rim light, blue/purple haze, and perfect spotlighting.

Material and heraldry:

- Dominant masonry is warm gray Crownlands fieldstone and dressed stone, visibly related across every scene.
- Timber is aged oak: beams, doors, furniture, stalls, racks, and roof structures should share craft language.
- Roof and visible exterior structures use clay tile, wood shingles, and practical repaired roofing, not glossy blue fantasy roofs.
- Heraldry uses restrained burgundy, faded blue, ochre, cream, and moss cloth with stitching, fading, and hand-painted marks.

Functional identity:

- Treasury: ledgers, scales, chests, locks, clerks, guards, and wax seals; restrained coins only.
- Great Hall: dais, long tables, hearth, tapestries, banners, narrow windows, carved wood.
- Barracks: racks, shields, spears, armor maintenance, benches, bunks or yard access, soldiers.
- Alehouse: benches, trestle tables, barrels, hearth, mugs, food, workers and retainers.
- Gatehouse: oak gate, portcullis, chains, alarm bell, guard room, wall tools, supplies.
- Royal Stables: horses, stalls, tack, saddles, hay, troughs, stable hands, courier gear.

Officer inheritance:

- Master of Coin inherits Treasury ledgers, keys, wax seals, scales, fine but sober cloth, and administrative authority.
- War Captain inherits Barracks practical military textiles, mail, weapon racks, campaign banners, and disciplined equipment.
- Defensive Commander inherits Gatehouse stone, iron, keys, command tokens, wall tools, helmets, and guard insignia.
- Cavalry Master inherits Royal Stables leather tack, courier bags, riding gear, horse blankets, mud, and stable daylight.

## Gear

Characters and all 32 gear pieces must follow one material culture:

- Barracks/War Captain: practical military equipment.
- Treasury/Master of Coin: administrative/economic equipment. The "weapon" slot is not a weapon; use ledger, abacus, coin scale, counting rod, royal quill, treasury scepter, or tax stamp.
- Royal Stables/Cavalry Master: riding, courier, horse, and road equipment.
- Gatehouse/Defensive Commander: fortification and defensive equipment.

### Officer And Common Gear Standards

The four Inner Castle officers are intentionally adult men. War Captain, Master of Coin, Cavalry Master, and Defensive Commander must remain clearly male in all future portrait revisions; do not create alternate or ambiguous-gender variants for these roles.

Canonical dimensions:

- Officer source master: 1086x1448 opaque RGB PNG in a 3:4 portrait frame.
- Officer optimized derivative: 768x1024 opaque WebP.
- Common Gear source master: 1254x1254 RGBA PNG on a standardized transparent square canvas.
- Common Gear optimized derivative: 192x192 RGBA WebP. Transparent padding is intentional and must not be tightly trimmed.
- Item silhouettes occupy roughly 68-84% of the safe area while retaining enough padding for slot borders and small-screen labels.

Portrait language:

- Full-body, three-quarter, human-scale portraits use the officer's Pass 3D building as their workplace and visual reference.
- All portraits share painterly naturalism, soft daylight with restrained practical interior warmth, realistic ambient shadow, and materially related Crownlands stone, oak, wool, linen, leather, dull iron, and muted brass.
- Faces remain distinct through age, structure, hair, beard, complexion, expression, and posture. Avoid modern hairstyles, photographic gloss, superhero anatomy, or cloned facial construction.
- Burgundy is the primary Crownlands authority accent. Faded blue, moss, charcoal, ochre, rust, and parchment may support it without becoming dominant fantasy livery.

Role identity:

- War Captain: weathered field officer, practical gambeson, mail, brigandine, campaign belt, restrained officer sword and muster orders.
- Master of Coin: prosperous educated administrator, quality wool and linen, ledger, seals, keys, scale, and sober status trim. He does not carry a conventional weapon.
- Cavalry Master: professional horseman and logistics officer, riding coat, rein gloves, tall boots, spurs, dispatch case, tack, and compact cavalry sword.
- Defensive Commander: veteran fortress officer with the heaviest practical armor, keys, shield, gate mechanisms, wall tools, and garrison authority.

Item language:

- Each eight-piece set is profession-specific rather than generic recolored armor.
- Treasury's weapon-slot art is permanently a non-weapon administrative tool. The current canonical object is a closed royal ledger with parchment tabs, wax seals, and attached brass counting scale.
- Gear icons are isolated, complete objects on transparent backgrounds, rendered at a shared camera and lighting treatment with no pedestal, frame, cast shadow, rarity glow, floating particles, text, or watermark.
- Equipment wear is functional: rubbed leather, tarnished metal, repaired seams, tool marks, stone dust, soot, and faded dye. Items remain maintained and useful.

## Animation

### High-Frequency Item And Reward Standards

- Common source canvas: 1254x1254 RGBA. Transparent framing is intentional and must remain fixed during optimization.
- Consumables use 160x160 RGBA WebP runtime canvases. Gold/troop pickups and the active Peace Shield field use 192x192 RGBA WebP runtime canvases. The Common Gear Box uses 192x192 closed art plus a 256x256 open reveal state.
- Objects use the Pass 3D/3E material culture: oak, iron, dull steel, leather, rope, linen, parchment, dark wax, muted brass, burgundy cloth, faded indigo, moss, ochre, and rust. Wear is practical and maintained.
- Shop and Bag presentations are merchant-ledger and quartermaster surfaces. Item silhouettes remain readable without rarity halos, square painted backdrops, or bright gold frames.
- Consumable identity is physical: painted shield and sealed proclamation; hide drum; sealed tax charter; coded cloth packet; courier dispatch case; practical signal horn.
- Gold rewards use a purse and restrained coin spill. Troop rewards use a helmet, compact banner, spear points, and muster roll. Both remain grounded objects rather than glowing resource symbols.
- Active Peace Shield protection uses a thin royal seal perimeter with wax markers and a mostly transparent center. City art, labels, selection states, and ownership marks stay visible beneath it.
- Reward motion uses a few readable physical tokens and a stamped arrival notation. Avoid particle showers, magical beams, and repeated neon pulses.
- Gear Box reveal is a physical sequence: latch moves, closed chest yields to the open state, then three parchment gear cards settle into place. Server-authoritative reward contents and timing remain independent from the animation.

Production prompts and negative constraints live in `docs/art-prompts/pass-3f/`.

Animations should feel physical:

- Banner movement, page turns, parchment unfurling, wax stamping.
- Coin scatter, dust, smoke, embers, sparks from steel.
- Wooden panel movement, cloth motion, army dust trails, gates opening, ink routes drawing.

Avoid generic glowing pulses where a physical medieval animation communicates the same thing.

## Accessibility And Mobile

Mobile landscape remains first priority. Preserve tap size, readable numbers, responsive layout, clear timers, and touch feedback. Reduced-motion mode must continue to work. Critical state needs color, shape, text, symbol, and/or pattern.

## Public Pages And Heraldry Standards

Public pages:

- Public navigation uses dark timber and iron depth with burgundy active/command states. Avoid glass blur, cyan links, oversized rounded cards and glossy gold buttons.
- Long-form Guides, Rules, Support, Privacy and update records use parchment/ledger reading surfaces with ink text, simple rules, restrained seals and no decorative framing that reduces readability.
- Home may use the approved Crownlands login landscape as an immersive background, but text must stay on the image rather than inside a floating marketing card.
- Desktop 1440x900, Android landscape 844x390 and portrait widths down to 390px are required public-page QA targets. Tables may scroll only when their contents cannot safely collapse.

Kingdom flags:

- The saved flag data model remains primary/accent hex, pattern and symbol. Display code may map approved values to cloth-dye treatments but must never rewrite existing saves merely for visual tone.
- Current dye presentation uses faded blue, burgundy red, moss green, faded indigo, ochre, charcoal, unbleached linen and walnut brown while preserving player differentiation.
- Cloth uses gentle woven lines, seam/shading, stitched edge and slight irregularity. Map flags must remain static and inexpensive.
- Charges use the Crownlands SVG pictogram family and remain readable at HUD/map scale.

Clan shields:

- Clan shields are painted wooden campaign shields rather than esports badges. Plank grain, worn paint, scratches and restrained forged edging provide material identity.
- Field divisions, charge layouts, colors, shape, trim and finish remain persistence-compatible. Visual wear must never reduce charge readability at roster or War Room size.
- Kingdom flags and clan shields share charge weight, dye culture and wear level, while retaining distinct cloth-banner and painted-shield silhouettes.

Secondary states:

- Empty states are parchment dispatch or ledger notes with a muted brass/timber rule.
- Errors use rust/oxblood warning edges with direct readable language; decoration never obscures recovery instructions.
- Editor-only tools use a restrained Crownlands administrative palette, but clarity, diagnostics and developer speed take precedence over historical ornament.

## Regional Map Runtime Delivery

- The editable 1448x1086 regional masters remain under `assets/worlds/world_01/maps/`. Runtime gameplay must use byte-identical content-fingerprinted copies under `assets/worlds/world_01/maps/versioned/`.
- `assets/worlds/world_01/map-manifest.json` is the delivery contract joining each region ID to its editable source, immutable runtime path, byte count, and SHA-256.
- Map UI previews and gameplay backgrounds must derive from the same restored source art. Preview and gameplay paths may differ for payload size, but their visual source may not diverge.
- Gameplay map URLs are immutable. New map bytes require a new fingerprint and build/cache version; an existing fingerprinted file must never be overwritten with different bytes.
- The production bundle ships immutable runtime maps, not mutable source-master URLs. PWA cache lookup must remain scoped to the active Crownlands cache so retired builds cannot satisfy current map requests.
- Regional art delivery changes must preserve dimensions, coordinates, blockers, roads, adjacency, routes, objectives, and all world topology.

## Things To Avoid

Avoid neon, magical blue/purple glow, crystal UI, luxury gold trim everywhere, perfect symmetry everywhere, glossy mobile-game cards, esports heraldry, generic dark gothic styling, skull/demon motifs, glowing swords, giant armor, and clean factory-perfect surfaces.

## Good Crownlands Treatment

- A city level button looks like a stamped steward ledger control with readable cost.
- An incoming attack appears as a torn dispatch with red wax and an oxblood cloth edge.
- A route line looks like ink drawn on a campaign map with a moving dust token.
- A reward claim uses a wax stamp and coin movement rather than sparkles.

## Bad Crownlands Treatment

- A glowing blue force field for peace shield.
- Bright gold borders around normal inventory slots.
- Perfect vector flags with no cloth.
- A fantasy castle token on empty terrain.
- Emoji telescope, sword, or crown as final iconography.
