Opponent colors warn you about kingdom strength

Light red, red, and dark red compare your whole kingdom’s King Power with another ruler’s. They do not compare only the troops inside the city you selected.

By the Crownlands Development TeamUpdated August 30, 20269 minute read

The short answer

Weak Player Protection limits attacks where the attacker’s King Power is at least 2.0 times the defender’s. It exists to reduce the damage that a much larger kingdom can do to a much smaller one while keeping ordinary rival cities part of the shared conflict.

A protected city is still normally attackable. The server limits how many troops can be sent and decides whether the order is a two-stage protected assault or a non-capturing protected raid. Red and dark-red opponents use normal King Power attack rules. Other protections can still block any of these attacks.

The server-confirmed attack screen is final. Map color is a strength warning, not a promise that an attack is legal. The server checks current King Power and every separate blocker when the order is submitted.

Read the three opponent colors

Light red #c9786f

Your King Power is at least 2.0 times the defender’s. Weak Player Protection applies. You can normally attack, but the server limits the force and chooses a protected assault or protected raid.

Red #b3261e

The defender is no stronger than you, but your King Power is below 2.0 times theirs. Normal attack, capture, and XP rules apply unless another protection blocks the city. A newly encountered city also begins red while confirmed power data is unavailable.

Dark red #4b1418

The defender has more King Power than you. Normal attack rules still apply, but the shade warns that the rival kingdom is stronger. City troops alone may not explain that difference.

When a city already has confirmed power data, Crownlands waits three seconds before changing it to another confirmed band. This prevents rapid color flicker. A recent King Power change or a newly loaded ruler can therefore leave a map shade briefly behind the server’s current decision.

Light red can mean two different battles

The color changes at 2.0 times King Power, but it does not tell you which protected battle is next. Open Attack and read the server-confirmed panel.

2.0× through below 2.5×: Protected Breach Assault

The first protected assault uses a server-calculated troop limit. If that assault wins, it can breach the wall but cannot capture the city. Only the same attacker’s follow-up against that same city and defender, during the same ownership period and in the same world, receives capture permission. A change of ownership invalidates the breach.

Exactly 2.5× and above: Protected Raid

A Protected Raid cannot capture. The server uses a lower troop limit, defender losses cannot exceed 10%, at least one defender remains, and wall damage does not persist. Every raider is lost with no Field Medics recovery, and the attacker receives 0 XP.

XP in every protected battle: the attacker earns 0 XP. The defender earns double XP for their first protected battle against that attacker in the current world, then normal defender XP for later protected battles against the same attacker.

Red and dark red use normal King Power rules

King Power measures the whole kingdom

The comparison uses each ruler’s server-authoritative King Power, not city level, the selected garrison, or a scout report. The current King Power model counts controlled troops, twelve hours of sustainable troop replacement, and twenty-five percent of defensive advantage.

Gold, raw city count, personal skills, Common Gear, and temporary items do not directly add King Power. Objective-supported production and defense can contribute through the replacement and defensive parts of the calculation. This is why two cities with similar visible troops can use different opponent shades.

A red shade does not guarantee an attack

These colors answer one question: how the two kingdoms compare for Weak Player Protection. Independent rules still decide whether the selected city can be attacked or scouted.

  • Main Cities cannot be attacked or scouted.
  • A Royal Peace Shield blocks player attacks while it remains active.
  • A clan ally cannot be attacked or scouted; the action becomes Reinforce.
  • Veil of Silence can hide a city and block scouting and targeting.
  • Server fair-play protections can block hostile attacks between a specific pair of accounts.

The server confirms protection when you launch

The server recalculates attack eligibility when the march is submitted. The accepted protection mode stays with that march even if either ruler’s King Power changes afterward. Defensive conditions remain live: troops, reinforcements, wall repair, objective support, and ownership can still change before arrival. A protected assault’s breach or capture stage is checked again when combat resolves.

If the attacker-to-defender ratio drops below 2.0 for a future order, that new attack returns to normal troop, capture, and XP rules. An older march already accepted as protected keeps its accepted mode.

The server derives the legal troop limit from current total defense and the attacker’s power per troop. Protected-assault scaling moves from 125% toward 105% of break-even. Raid scaling moves from 50% at 2.5× to 25% at 5×, then remains at 25% above 5×. The exposed limits use the implementation’s two-significant-digit rounding rules.

Where Weak Player Protection does not apply

Weak Player Protection applies only to ordinary player-owned cities. It does not apply to neutral cities, Reward Camps, Strongholds, the Crown Citadel, transfers between your own cities, or ordinary objective Rallies. Ordinary Rallies target Strongholds and the Crown Citadel; regular cities and Reward Camps are not valid Rally targets.

When a friendly march becomes hostile

A transfer, clan reinforcement, or Rally return can find that ownership changed while it was moving. The server may then evaluate the converted hostile arrival under current protection. Excess troops above the protected limit are returned. A formerly owned transfer that converts into an attack can capture if it wins even when classified as a raid; a converted clan reinforcement does not receive that capture exception.

These retargeting exceptions do not change what the map colors mean. They exist because the march began as a different order and became hostile only after the destination changed.

Four checks before you attack

  1. Use the shade to understand the whole-kingdom strength comparison.
  2. If the city is light red, open Attack to see whether the server confirms a protected assault or raid.
  3. Check for a Main City, Peace Shield, clan relationship, Veil of Silence, or fair-play block.
  4. Read the accepted troop limit and outcome text before confirming the march.
